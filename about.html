The Conservator

About Me

Preserving Art. Safeguarding History.

Art has fascinated me for as long as I can remember — not only for its beauty, but above all for the stories it tells. Artworks mirror people, cultures and eras, and continue to shape our understanding of history today.

Studying art history was therefore the natural first step for me. The more deeply I engaged with artworks and their historical significance, the greater my enthusiasm for our cultural heritage grew.

The turning point came during an internship in Dresden. I watched a colleague examine a painting that had been taken out of its frame and laid on the work table. Suddenly, the artwork was no longer just a finished picture — through its materials, brushstrokes, varnish layers and earlier restorations, it told an entirely new story.

In that moment, I realised that conservator-restorers are, in many ways, detectives of art. Every crack, every paint layer, every alteration offers clues to an artwork's history. Reading and understanding these traces, and drawing the best possible conservation decisions from them, fascinated me from that very first moment.

Today, it's exactly this combination of art, history, science and hands-on precision that excites me. Every artwork raises new questions, every object tells its own story — and that is what makes this profession so special.

To me, the task of a conservator is not simply to restore artworks, but to understand them, preserve their authenticity, and safeguard them for future generations.

Education

Master of Arts in Conservation-Restoration

During my Master's degree, I specialised in the conservation and restoration of paintings and sculptures. My thesis examined a group of works by the Swiss Renaissance painter Hans Asper. By combining technical art examination, conservation research and art-historical analysis, I was able to gain new insights into painting technique, materiality and the works' history. The thesis was developed in close collaboration with the Swiss National Museum's Collection Centre and formed the basis for a scientific publication.

Bachelor of Arts in Art History

My studies in art history laid the foundation for my understanding of art, cultural history and visual analysis — knowledge that remains a key foundation of my conservation work today.

My Philosophy

To me, conservation means far more than treating visible damage.

At the heart of my work is the long-term preservation of an artwork's original substance. The goal is to slow ageing processes, stabilise damage and preserve an object's authenticity. Restoration measures are carried out only where they are necessary for an artwork's stability, legibility or long-term preservation.

Every conservation decision begins with a careful understanding of the artwork — its materials, its history, and the changes it has undergone over time. There is no standard solution. Every object deserves an individual approach, grounded in scientific examination, professional experience and the greatest respect for its history.

My work follows the professional code of ethics of the European Confederation of Conservator-Restorers' Organisations (ECCO). These internationally recognised guidelines place respectful treatment of original substance, scientifically grounded decisions, thorough documentation, and responsibility for the long-term preservation of our cultural heritage at their core.

Outlook
